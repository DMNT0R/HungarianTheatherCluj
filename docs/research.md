## 1 · Rhédey Palace (1792)

- **Location:** Piața Unirii 9, corner of Str. Napoca; today the *Stâlpii Împușcați*
  (“shot pillars”) memorial of the 1989 revolution stands at this corner [ADC-2].
  OSM building “Palatul Rhédey”: 46.76873, 23.58938 [OSM]. Some sources say “today's
  Str. Napoca” [ADC-3].
- Converted into a Baroque palace in 1775–77 by Mihály Rhédey or his wife, Countess
  Terézia Bánffy (search result summary, explorecarpathia.eu – not read directly ⚠️).
- October 1792: the noble young actors led by János Fejér (József Kontz, János Sáska,
  Pál Jantsó, Mihály Verestói, István and Rozália Fejér, Terézia Kis, Terézia Keszeg)
  petition for the right to perform in Hungarian [HT-200, HT-230].
- Countess Rhédey (née Baroness Terézia Bánffy) lends her ballroom free for a year
  [HT-200].
- First performance *A titkos ellenkezés vagy Köleséri* on **17 December 1792** [HT-232].
  ⚠️ HT-200 also says 16 December in one place; an older tradition gives 11 November
  1792 – Sándor Enyedi (1982) moved it to 17 December on the basis of new sources.
- The ballroom was the company's occasional home 1792–1821 [WP-KAMSZ].

## 2 · Birthplace of King Matthias

- OSM building “Casa Matia”: 46.77113, 23.58756 [OSM].
- Link to the theatre: on 13 March 1821 the professional company opened its first
  permanent house with László Szentjóbi Szabó's *Mátyás király* [HT-200].

## 3 · The first stone theatre, Farkas utca (1821–1906)

- **Site:** corner of Farkas utca (today Str. Mihail Kogălniceanu) and *Színház utca*
  (“Theatre Street”, today Str. Emmanuel de Martonne) [ADC-3]. Today the **Colegiul
  Academic** of Babeș-Bolyai University with the **Auditorium Maximum** hall stands on
  the old foundations [ADC-2, ADC-4, HT-230]. OSM “Auditorium Maximum”,
  Str. Mihail Kogălniceanu 5: 46.76772, 23.59252 [OSM].
- Plot bought from the Reformed College on 13 July 1803 by five aristocrats; building
  started 1803, **stopped 1807 for lack of money, resumed 1811**, finished 1821; the plan
  was modelled on the theatre in Pozsony/Bratislava [ADC-3].
- Architect **Antal Alföldi** [HT-200, ADC-3]. ⚠️ ADC-3 also names “Antal Schütz”.
- **800 seats, 50 boxes** (15 + 17 + 18 on three levels); hall lit by a chandelier with
  12 oil lamps, the stage by **200 candles**; orchestra pit for 19 musicians. Lázár Káli
  Nagy: 800 comfortably, 1,200 full, 1,400 “so tightly that there is no room left”
  [ADC-3].
- Opening 12/13 March 1821: aristocrats in Körner's *Zrínyi*, then the professionals in
  *Mátyás király*; the aristocrats donated their costumes to the theatre [HT-200].
- **József Katona wrote *Bánk bán*** – later the Hungarian national drama – for the play
  competition announced for this theatre's opening [WP-KAMSZ]. Its Cluj premiere was in
  1834, with Déryné as Melinda [HT-200].
- First permanent theatre building in the whole Hungarian-speaking area [ADC-1].
- Careers that began here: Mari Jászai (1869), Ede Paulay, Imre Szacsvay [ADC-1, HT-200].
- January 1899: Jenő Janovics first used **film scenes inside a stage play** here
  [ADC-1, HT-200].
- Last performance 7 September 1906 [HT-200]. Afterwards bought by the state for the
  university, used as a depot of the botanical museum after 1918, demolished in the
  1930s [HT-200: 1935]. New building by architect George Cristinel, built 1934–36,
  **inaugurated 13 June 1937 by King Carol II**; names: Colegiul “Regele Carol II”,
  student house “Mátyás király/Regele Matia” (1940–44), **Casa Universitarilor** (from
  1950), today Colegiul Academic; home of the Transilvania Philharmonic's concerts since
  1955 [ADC-4].
- Memorial plaque for the 200th anniversary on the wall [HT-200]; exhibition *Noble
  Traces* (2022, Budapest; later shown in the theatre's upstairs foyer) included a piece
  of the Farkas Street stage floor that **Janovics built into the stage of the new
  Hunyadi Square theatre as a symbol of continuity** [HT-EXPO, HT-232].

## 4 · Házsongárd Cemetery

- Founded 11 May 1585 by the town council during a plague epidemic, on the town's
  melon field outside the Torda Street gate; open to everyone regardless of religion
  or ethnicity; no free plots since 1979 [WP-HAZS].
- OSM centre of the cemetery: 46.76266, 23.59300 ⚠️ (better: the main gate – to check).
- **Graves of theatre people:**
  - **Jenő Janovics** (1872–1945): grave in section II. A., listed monument
    (CJ-IV-m-B-07839.052), **on the site of the grave of actress Anikó Székelyné Ungár**
    [WP-JANOVICS].
  - Pál Jantsó Gidófalvi (1761–1845) with Celesztin Pergő (1784–1858) next to him
    [HT-200].
  - Mária Néb (1799–1884) [HT-200, photo of the tomb].
  - Anikó Székelyné Ungár (1796–1865 per HT-200) – her original grave site now holds
    Janovics [WP-JANOVICS]. ⚠️ A burial database gives 1790–1862.

## 5 · National Theatre on Hunyadi Square (1906)

- Fellner & Helmer, opened 8 September 1906; Janovics director since April 1905 (first
  production: *Cyrano de Bergerac*, still in Farkas utca) [WP-JANOVICS].
- **21 December 1918:** with Romanian troops about 20 km away, Janovics gathers some
  250 staff on stage at dawn: *“As long as I breathe, I will defend this theatre that I
  was assigned to manage.”* His wife, actress **Lili Poór**, answers *“Itt maradunk!”*
  (“We stay here!”), and everyone joins in [ENYEDI].
- 14 May 1919: Onisifor Ghibu, on behalf of the Governing Council, orders the takeover;
  the same day the Bucharest National Theatre plays there – the first Romanian words on
  that stage: *“S-a potolit furtuna…”* (“The storm has calmed…”) [ENYEDI].
- Janovics argued the building was not state property (built on a bank loan he repaid
  from a state subsidy; lease contract until 1921); he also offered to hand it over if he
  could buy or rent the old Farkas Street theatre – no answer [ENYEDI, WP-JANOVICS].
- **30 September 1919, last Hungarian performance:** *Hamlet*, Janovics in the title role,
  Lili Poór as Ophelia. The censors cut the great monologue to its first line; when
  Janovics addressed “To be, or not to be” to the audience, a woman shouted “We want to
  live!” and about 2,000 voices joined in. The last Hungarian words on that stage were
  Hamlet's dying lines. Police cleared the house [ENYEDI, WP-KAMSZ].
- 1 October 1919, 10 a.m.: the building passes to the Romanian National Theatre under
  Zaharia Bârsan [ENYEDI].
- **Balance for a Romanian audience:** before 1918 Romanian theatre in Transylvania had
  been obstructed (permits for “non-Hungarian” actors; the 1907 Apponyi laws; no Romanian
  touring company is known to have reached Cluj 1906–1913); for Romanians the takeover was
  “a matter of national pride” [ENYEDI].
- **1940–1945** (after the Second Vienna Award) the Hungarian company played in this
  building again; Janovics directed it once more from 20 January to 21 October 1945
  [WP-KAMSZ, WP-JANOVICS].
- Today: Lucian Blaga National Theatre and Romanian National Opera. OSM: 46.77013,
  23.59749 [OSM].

## 6 · Film: Janovics, Kertész, Korda

- Janovics shot street scenes as early as 1907; film company from 1912/1913; in 1913 *The
  Yellow Foal* (*Sárga csikó*), co-produced with Pathé, became an international success
  [WP-JANOVICS]. ⚠️ Company names and dates (Corvin, Proja, Transsylvania) differ between
  sources – avoid them in the narration.
- 1914–1920: **65 silent films** and 5 cinema sketches; he owned 26 cinemas, 11 of them
  in Kolozsvár, and used the profits to fund the theatre [WP-JANOVICS]. Only four of
  these films survive complete (search result, Wikipedia “Corvin Film Studio” ⚠️).
- He brought **Mihály Kertész** (Michael Curtiz), then Márton Garas, then for a year
  **Sándor Korda** (Sir Alexander Korda) to Kolozsvár – Korda “laid the foundation of his
  world fame here” [WP-JANOVICS].
- 1914 *Bánk bán* directed by Kertész, with Mari Jászai as the Queen [HT-200]; exteriors at
  the Kornis castle in Szentbenedek [WP-JANOVICS].
- **The studio stood in the courtyard of the summer theatre** – a simple glass-roofed
  structure; Janovics also converted the summer theatre into a cinema (search result
  summary of Delia Enyedi's SCIA article ⚠️); a silent film was shot in the Central Park
  (photo caption of Lili Poór, same source ⚠️).
- Station 6 location: Central Park, about 160 m before the theatre (46.77138, 23.58308)
  – chosen because exteriors were shot in the park and the studio was next door.

## 7 · The summer theatre – today's Hungarian Theatre

- A wooden summer theatre at the park entrance from **1874** [ADC-2, ENYEDI]; replaced
  1908/09–1910 by the brick building, **Secessionist style, architects Géza Márkus and
  Frigyes Spiegel**; Janovics paid for the building himself [WP-KAMSZ, WP-JANOVICS,
  ENYEDI]. Without heating when the company moved in for the winter of 1919/20 [ENYEDI].
- Season opened there on **4 October 1919** (search result ⚠️).
- Home of the company 1919–1940 and since 1945; rebuilt **1959–61** (architects Ioan Paiu
  and János Sóvágó); main hall with **862 seats**; Hungarian Opera shares it; new studio
  hall on the Someș bank since 2008 [WP-KAMSZ].
- Address: Str. Emil Isac 26–28 – named after the Romanian poet **Emil Isac**, who in 1919
  was one of the officials in charge of cultural policy in Transylvania [ENYEDI].
- Janovics under Romanian rule: staged Romanian authors (Caragiale, Eftimiu, Blaga, Iorga),
  took the company on tour to Bucharest, ran playwriting competitions (1923, 1924, 1930 –
  Áron Tamási took part) and was attacked by nationalists of both sides; in 1923 he
  declined the directorship of the National Theatre in Budapest: *“I cannot cut the ties
  that bind me to Kolozsvár.”* [WP-JANOVICS, HT-230]
- Key directors: Gyula E. Kovács (Shakespeare cycle), Jenő Janovics, György Harag (chief
  director 1973/75–1985, “a great Maestro of the theatre in Romania”), Gábor Tompa (since
  1990) [HT-HIST, WP-KAMSZ].
- Member of the Union of the Theatres of Europe since 2008; performances surtitled in
  Romanian and English [HT-ABOUT: huntheater.ro/en/the-theatre/about-us/].

## Additions from the team's material (25 Nov 2026)

**Film [ENYEDI-21, NFI]**
- *The Yellow Foal* (1913) was distributed in 137 copies to almost 40 countries [ENYEDI-21].
- Janovics supervised **66** silent feature films 1913–1920 [ENYEDI-21]; WP-JANOVICS says 65 films
  (1914–1920) plus 5 sketches ⚠️ → the app says “more than sixty”.
- Curtiz and Korda's careers “originated on the streets of Cluj as Mihály Kertész and Sándor
  László Kellner” [ENYEDI-21].
- **A tolonc** (1914): directed by Mihály Kertész, script by Jenő Janovics, camera László Fekete,
  starring Lili Berky, Mihály Várkonyi, **Mari Jászai**, István Szentgyörgyi; produced by Proja;
  shot June–July 1914 in Kolozsvár, Torockó, the Turda Gorge and on the Arieș; **the only
  surviving film with Mari Jászai**; an English-language copy was found in the basement of the
  Hungarian House in New York; shown in Vienna and Berlin; full film on filmio.hu [NFI].
- Last Transylvania studio feature: *Menace* (*Világrém*, 1920), a social-hygiene film on syphilis
  ordered by the Romanian Ministry of Health, shot in Cluj hospitals, premiere 31 Dec 1920; studio
  closed after 1924 [ENYEDI-21].
- 1934/35 Janovics proposed a national film industry to the Romanian government; 1935–41 he made
  documentaries for both Romanian and Hungarian clients [ENYEDI-21].

**Buildings and dates [KOCSIS] – to verify**
- Rhédey Palace: formerly “Chain House” (*Casa cu lanțuri*); used by the company 1792–93 and again
  1819–21; **commemorative plaque on the façade** (used in the app, stop 1). First play's author:
  Sándor Kövesdi Boér. *Hamlet* on 23 Nov 1793 (venue unverified).
- Farkas Street: present address 1 Emmanuel de Martonne Street; *Béla futása* 26 Dec 1822; Cluj
  premiere of *Bánk bán* **30 Aug 1834** (Lendvay as Bánk, Egressy as Otto) ⚠️ ADC-1 says 6 Sep 1834;
  farewell *Bánk bán* 17 June 1906; 1885 recognised as second National Theatre.
- First summer theatre: opened **13 June 1874**, architect Henrik Zimmermann, financed by intendant
  Bogdán Korbuly, demolished 1908.
- National Theatre: built 1904–06; Romanian National Theatre opened 1–2 Dec 1919 (*Se face ziuă*,
  *Ovidiu*); Romanian Opera 25 May 1920 (*Aida*); Hungarian performances returned **Nov 1941**
  ⚠️ (WP-KAMSZ: 1940–45; the app says “between 1940 and 1945”).
- Summer theatre 1910: opened **18 July 1910** (some studies: 18 Aug) ⚠️; reinforced-concrete dome of
  about 28 m; 1959–61 rebuild substantially changed the façade; Hungarian Opera independent from
  11 Dec 1948 (*Háry János*).
- The team's wish list of archive material includes **“comparative photographs: façade and
  square in 1906 and today”** – now implemented as the *Then & Now* camera.
